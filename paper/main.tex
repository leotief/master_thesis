\begin{abstract}
  Probabilistic circuits (PCs) compute marginals and conditionals exactly, which makes them a natural model for data with missing features, yet they are rarely combined with a logistic regression, because the sigmoid destroys their tractability.
  We show that the expected prediction of a logistic regression under a PC, marginalised over the missing features, can be computed deterministically and differentiably.
  Rewriting the sigmoid with the Pólya--Gamma and Hubbard--Stratonovich identities reduces it to the conditional characteristic function of the PC, which a smooth and decomposable circuit computes in one pass, and to two one-dimensional integrals, for which we construct a Gaussian quadrature rule for the Pólya--Gamma distribution.
  The PC and the logistic regression thus form one joint model with separate parameters, in which the classifier is trained discriminatively on incomplete data.
  At the same budget our estimator is about four orders of magnitude more accurate than Monte Carlo and trains the same classifier as the exact expectation.
\end{abstract}
